## Why a single hourly rate undersells you

A single number ("$150/hour") invites exactly one comparison: is that expensive or cheap. A tiered rate card invites a different question: which of these fits me — a framing that consistently produces a higher average sale, because the client is choosing a package, not negotiating a number.

## Structure of a strong rate card

### Three tiers, not one price
A common, effective structure:
- **Starter** — a narrowly scoped, lower-cost entry point (e.g., a single audit or focused deliverable)
- **Standard** — your core offer, the one most clients should land on, priced to be your default engagement
- **Premium** — an expanded version with more scope, more touchpoints, or faster turnaround, priced meaningfully higher

Anchor the premium tier high enough that the standard tier reads as the reasonable middle choice — most buyers gravitate to the middle option when given three.

### What's included, itemized per tier
List concrete inclusions per tier (number of revisions, turnaround time, deliverables, support window) — not just a price. Buyers compare tiers on what's included, not only cost.

### A stated starting price, not a bare hourly rate
Even "Starting at $X" gives a prospect enough information to self-qualify before booking a call — filtering out mismatched inquiries that would otherwise cost you unpaid discovery calls.

### Add-ons
A short list of common extras (rush delivery, an extra revision round, an additional deliverable) priced individually — captures upsell revenue on projects that started at the base tier.

### A single, clear CTA
"Book a call" or "Send a project brief" — one next step, not three competing options that create decision friction at the exact moment you want a lead to act.

## How to price each tier without guessing

Start from your day rate (calculate yours free with the Day Rate Calculator), then price each tier as a specific number of days of work plus a margin for packaging and risk — not as a round number pulled from a competitor's website. A rate card built on your actual numbers is one you can defend on a sales call without hesitating.
