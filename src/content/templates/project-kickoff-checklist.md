## Why the first week decides the timeline

By the time a project feels late, the cause is almost always something that should have happened in week one and didn't: an access request that sat in an inbox, a decision-maker who was never identified, brand assets that turned out not to be final. A kickoff checklist exists to move all of that friction to the front, deliberately, while everyone's attention and enthusiasm are highest — instead of discovering the gaps one blocked afternoon at a time.

## The four things a kickoff has to lock down

### Access
List every system, account, tool, and repository you'll need, and request all of them at once on day one. Logins, admin invites, shared drives, staging environments, analytics, design files. Access requests have the longest lead time of anything in a project because they depend on someone else's IT, calendar, or password manager — so they belong first, not when you happen to hit the wall.

### Assets
Get every input in hand and confirm it's *final*. Brand guidelines, copy, logos, data exports, existing files. The dangerous phrase here is "we'll send that over" — an asset that's promised but not delivered is a dependency, and dependencies belong in the [scope of work](/templates/scope-of-work-template) with a name attached, not floating in a Slack thread.

### People and decisions
Name the single point of contact for day-to-day questions and the actual decision-maker who signs off (they're often not the same person). Confirm who can approve deliverables and who can approve a [change order](/templates/change-order-template). Projects stall for weeks when feedback arrives from three people who disagree and no one is empowered to break the tie.

### Cadence
Set the communication rhythm before you need it: how you'll update the client (a [weekly client update](/templates/weekly-client-update-template) is the low-effort default), which channel is for what, and how quickly each side commits to responding. Ambiguous cadence is where "I thought you were waiting on me" delays are born.

## Run it right after the questionnaire

The [client onboarding questionnaire](/templates/client-onboarding-questionnaire) comes first — it's how you learn enough to scope. The kickoff checklist comes after the contract is signed, and it's operational: the concrete list of things you need in hand to start building. Pair it with a short kickoff call so the point of contact, the decision-maker, and the cadence are all confirmed out loud, and use the [SOW generator](/tools/sow-generator) to turn the scope you agreed on into the document the kickoff references. Do this well and week one produces momentum instead of a queue of things you're waiting on.
