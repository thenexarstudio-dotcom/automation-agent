## Why the end of a project is worth more than most freelancers realize

Most freelance engagements end the same way: final deliverable sent, final invoice sent, silence. That's a missed opportunity — the moment right after a successful delivery is the single highest-leverage moment in the entire client relationship for referrals, testimonials, and repeat work, and it only exists for a short window.

## The checklist

### 1. Confirm final delivery and approval
Get explicit written confirmation the client considers the final deliverable complete and accepted — protects against later disputes and is the natural trigger for the rest of this list.

### 2. Send the final invoice
Reference the Invoice Template, itemized against the SOW, sent the same day as approval — not batched with other admin later.

### 3. Request a testimonial
Ask specifically, before the final payment lands: name 1-2 concrete outcomes you'd like them to speak to, rather than a generic "would you write me a review." Specific prompts produce specific, usable testimonials.

### 4. Request a referral
A short, specific ask (see FAQ above) — the majority of the best freelance clients come from a direct referral, not from cold outreach or SEO alone, which makes this the highest-ROI five minutes in the entire engagement.

### 5. Deliver final assets and access handoff
Transfer files, revoke or hand back any access you were given, and confirm the client has everything they need without you — this is also the point where IP ownership formally transfers per the Service Agreement.

### 6. Propose next steps, if relevant
If there's a natural follow-on (maintenance, a phase two, a retainer), name it explicitly rather than assuming the client will ask — many don't think to ask if you don't raise it first.

### 7. Internal debrief note
Two minutes, for yourself: what went well, what you'd price differently next time, what to add to your own Scope of Work template going forward. This is how your own documents (and your pricing) improve engagement over engagement.

## The pattern

None of these steps take long individually — the reason they get skipped is that there's no checklist prompting them at the exact moment a project naturally winds down. Attaching this checklist to your own "final delivery" step turns it into a habit instead of an afterthought.
