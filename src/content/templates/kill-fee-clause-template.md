## Why "pay for work completed" isn't enough

Most freelance contracts already say that if a client cancels early, they owe you for work completed to date. That's necessary, but it quietly assumes the only cost of cancellation is the hours you already logged. It ignores the bigger cost: you likely turned down other work, blocked off calendar time, or shaped your pipeline around this engagement continuing. A kill fee clause is what compensates for that — a defined cancellation payment on top of work-to-date, specifically because a client walking away mid-project costs you more than the invoice for finished deliverables shows.

## The two structures this template covers

### Percentage of remaining fee
The client pays a set percentage — commonly 10-25% — of whatever was left unbilled on the contract at the point of cancellation. This scales naturally with project size and is the simplest structure to negotiate, since it's a single number applied against a total the client already agreed to.

### Work-to-date plus a flat cancellation fee
The client pays for all completed work as normal, plus a separate flat fee (or a fee tied to the notice period given) for the cancellation itself. This structure reads more clearly to clients who dislike percentage math, and works well when paired with a [milestone payment schedule](/templates/milestone-payment-schedule-template) — the kill fee simply becomes another line calculated once cancellation is confirmed.

## What the clause needs to specify

### Trigger and notice period
Define what counts as a cancellation (as opposed to a pause or a scope reduction) and the minimum notice the client must give — commonly 14-30 days. A longer notice period can reduce or waive the kill fee entirely, which gives clients a real incentive to tell you early rather than disappear.

### The fee structure and amount
State plainly which of the two structures applies, the percentage or flat amount, and how it's calculated against the remaining contract value. Vague language here ("a reasonable cancellation fee") defeats the purpose — it should be a number either side can compute without a conversation.

### Timing of payment
When the kill fee is due relative to the cancellation notice — typically alongside the final invoice for work completed, so it's one clean settlement rather than a second negotiation.

**A note on the legal side:** this clause is a well-structured starting point covering common freelance cancellation practice, not legal advice. Have a lawyer review it before relying on it, and confirm cancellation and kill fee provisions are enforceable in your jurisdiction and appropriate for the size of engagement you're using it on.

## Where this clause sits in your document stack

A kill fee clause isn't a standalone document — it's meant to slot directly into Section 7 (Termination) of your [freelance service agreement](/templates/freelance-contract-template), sitting alongside the existing "pay for work completed" language rather than replacing it. It's especially worth adding to [retainer agreements](/templates/retainer-agreement-template), where an early cancellation can end recurring income you'd planned your pipeline around with very little warning.
