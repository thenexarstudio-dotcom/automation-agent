## Why "we're done" needs a document

Most freelance engagements end with a final deliverable and a final invoice, and the actual results — what changed, what it was worth, what you'd recommend next — live only in your head or scattered across old emails. That's a lost asset. A project completion report closes the loop in writing: it tells the client (and anyone they answer to) exactly what was delivered and what it achieved, and it gives you a reusable record for your own case studies and future pricing conversations.

## What belongs in a completion report

### A short executive summary
Two or three sentences a busy executive could read and understand the entire engagement's value from — written for someone who wasn't in any of your meetings.

### Original objectives, restated
Pull this directly from your [scope of work](/templates/scope-of-work-template) so there's no ambiguity about what success was supposed to look like. Restating the original goal before reporting on it is what makes the results section land as "we did what we said," not just a list of activity.

### Deliverables completed
An itemized list or table mirroring your SOW's deliverables, each marked complete with a one-line note — this is the section a client scans first to confirm nothing was missed.

### Results and impact
Wherever you have numbers — time saved, revenue impact, conversion change, error reduction — put them here, before and after where possible. This is the section that gets forwarded upward, and it's the single best source of material for your next [testimonial or referral request](/templates/testimonial-referral-request-scripts).

### Challenges and how they were resolved
A brief, honest account of what got in the way and what you did about it. This does more for your credibility than a report with no friction in it at all — it shows judgment, not just output.

### Recommendations and next steps
Name what you'd do next if the engagement continued — this is where a completion report quietly becomes a retainer pitch or a phase-two proposal, without ever sounding like a pitch.

## How to use it well

Send the completion report at the same moment you'd normally send a [weekly-style update](/templates/weekly-client-update-template) for the final week — before the offboarding conversation, not buried inside it. It reads best as a standalone artifact the client can forward without editing, which is exactly the point: you want it circulating in their organization making the case for you when you're not in the room.

## Where this fits in the rest of the Kit

The completion report is the evidence-gathering step that makes everything after it easier — it feeds directly into the [offboarding checklist](/templates/offboarding-checklist)'s testimonial and referral asks, because you're pulling specific, already-written results instead of trying to remember them from memory a week later.
