## Why "just one small thing" is the most expensive sentence in freelancing

Individually, small out-of-scope requests feel too minor to push back on. Collectively, across a project, they're one of the largest sources of unbilled hours freelancers work. A change order isn't about saying no — it's about making every "yes" carry its actual cost and time, in writing, before the work starts.

## What goes in it

### Reference to the original agreement
Name the original SOW/contract this change order modifies, and the date, so it's clearly an amendment rather than a standalone ambiguous request.

### Description of the change
Plain language: what's being added, changed, or removed relative to the original scope. Specific enough that both sides would describe it the same way afterward.

### Impact on deliverables
State exactly what's being added to (or removed from) the original deliverables list.

### Impact on timeline
A specific new date, or a specific number of added days — not "this might push things back a bit."

### Impact on price
The exact additional (or reduced) fee, and whether it's due immediately, added to the next invoice, or billed on completion.

### Approval
A signature/checkbox line for the client to approve before the additional work begins. This is the step that converts a request into a paid, agreed-upon addition rather than a favor.

## How to use it in the moment

When a client asks for something outside the SOW, the reflex to build is: acknowledge it positively, then say "let me send you a quick change order so we can get this scheduled properly" — same day, not a week later. Speed here matters: a same-day change order feels like responsiveness; a change order that shows up after you've already done the work feels like a bill for something they thought was included.

## Where this fits

This template only works because the Scope of Work Template defined a clear baseline to change *from*. Without a specific SOW, there's no clean line between "included" and "additional" — which is exactly the gap that turns freelance projects into unpaid scope creep.
