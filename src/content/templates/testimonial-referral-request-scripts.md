## Why good clients don't leave testimonials

It's almost never unwillingness. A happy client who'd genuinely recommend you still doesn't write a testimonial because the request they got was impossible to act on: "It'd mean a lot if you could say something" gives them a blank page, no angle, and no deadline, so it slides to next week forever. The fix isn't more enthusiasm on your end — it's a better-engineered ask. Specific, low-effort, and timed to the moment their satisfaction is highest.

## The three levers of a request that gets answered

### Timing
The window is narrow and it's early. The best moment for both testimonials and referrals is right after final delivery is approved and before the final invoice clears — the exact moment covered by the [offboarding checklist](/templates/offboarding-checklist). Wait until the project is a memory and you're asking someone to reconstruct enthusiasm they no longer feel.

### Specificity
Name the outcome you want them to speak to. "Would you be willing to say a few words about how the launch went?" is answerable in a way "would you write me a review?" never is. For referrals, describe the exact person: "one or two other [role] dealing with [the problem you just solved]." A specific filter gets run; a vague one gets ignored.

### Effort
Remove the blank page. Offer to draft the testimonial from what they've already told you, and offer to write the referral intro email so all they do is forward it. The lower the effort you're asking for, the higher the response rate — and a draft they edit reads just as genuine as one they wrote cold.

## Where these fit

These scripts are the words for the moments the [offboarding checklist](/templates/offboarding-checklist) tells you to hit. A testimonial you collect at delivery becomes social proof in your next [consulting proposal](/templates/consulting-proposal-template) and on your site; a referral becomes your highest-ROI source of new work, since a warm introduction skips the entire trust-building phase that cold outreach has to earn. Keep the scripts in your snippets so the ask is a thirty-second paste at the right moment, not a task you talk yourself out of. The scripts cover four situations — the testimonial ask, the draft-it-for-them variant, the referral ask, and a light re-ask for when the first request goes quiet — each phrased to be specific enough to answer and easy enough to say yes to.
