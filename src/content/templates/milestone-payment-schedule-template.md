## Why a fixed price still needs a payment schedule

Quoting a flat project fee doesn't remove the cash-flow risk that hourly billing avoids — it just moves the risk to a different question: *when* does that fee get paid? "50% up front, 50% on completion" is the default freelancers reach for, and it's better than nothing, but it still leaves the entire back half of a project unpaid until the very end. A milestone payment schedule breaks the total fee into smaller payments tied to specific points of progress, so you're never more than one milestone's worth of work away from getting paid.

## What the schedule needs to specify

### Deposit
A non-refundable or partially-refundable payment due before work begins — commonly 25-50% of the total fee. The deposit does two jobs: it funds your first weeks of work, and it filters out clients who aren't serious enough to commit money before you commit time.

### Milestones tied to deliverables, not dates
Each milestone should trigger on something check-able — "wireframes delivered," "first draft approved," "final files delivered" — not a calendar date divorced from progress. Pull the milestone names directly from the deliverables list in your [scope of work](/templates/scope-of-work-template) so there's no ambiguity about what has to be true for a payment to become due. A schedule that pays out on dates regardless of progress protects the client's cash flow at the expense of yours.

### Amount per milestone
State a specific dollar amount (or percentage of the total fee) next to each milestone, not just a proportion you'll calculate later. Front-load slightly where you can — a schedule weighted toward early milestones reduces your exposure if a project stalls or a client disengages partway through.

### Approval window and trigger date
Define how a milestone gets marked complete — client sign-off, or a set number of days after delivery with no objection (a "deemed approved" clause protects you from a milestone sitting in limbo because a client went quiet). State the number of days you have to invoice after a milestone triggers, and the payment terms that apply from there (matching your [service agreement](/templates/freelance-contract-template)).

### What happens if the project stalls
Name what happens to a milestone payment if the client pauses or cancels before it's reached — typically, you invoice for the percentage of that milestone's work completed to date. This is where the schedule intersects with your contract's [kill fee clause](/templates/kill-fee-clause-template), if one is in place.

## How this fits with the rest of the Kit

The milestone payment schedule is not a substitute for the [invoice template](/templates/invoice-template) — it's the plan; the invoice is the execution. Once a milestone triggers, you generate an invoice referencing that specific line of the schedule, using the itemized format the invoice template lays out. And because the schedule only works if scope stays fixed, tie it explicitly to the [scope of work](/templates/scope-of-work-template): if the [scope creep cost calculator](/tools/scope-creep-cost-calculator) shows a request has grown past what a milestone covers, that's your signal to route it through a change order before continuing, not to quietly absorb it into the next payment.
