## What a freelance service agreement actually needs to do

A good contract isn't about anticipating litigation — it's about making the boring, predictable moments (a late payment, a client going quiet, a project ending early) resolve by reference to a document instead of by negotiation under stress. This template covers the clauses that come up most often in freelance and consulting engagements.

## The clauses this template includes

### Parties and engagement summary
Who's engaged, effective date, and a one-line reference to the attached Scope of Work as the definition of what's being delivered — keeping the contract itself short and the deliverable detail in one place (the SOW).

### Payment terms
Fee, currency, invoicing schedule (e.g., "50% deposit, 50% on delivery" or milestone-based), accepted payment methods, and — critically — what happens on late payment (a specific late fee percentage and a defined point at which work pauses). Vague payment terms are the single most common source of freelancer cash-flow stress.

### Intellectual property / ownership
When ownership of the work transfers to the client — commonly "upon receipt of final payment," not upon delivery. This single line is what protects you if a client tries to use deliverables before paying in full.

### Confidentiality
A mutual, reasonably scoped confidentiality clause — protects client information you're exposed to, and protects your own methods/tools/templates from being treated as the client's property.

### Independent contractor status
An explicit statement that you're an independent contractor, not an employee — relevant for tax treatment and for making clear neither side owes the other benefits or employment protections.

### Termination
Notice period for either party to end the engagement early, and what's owed for work completed up to that point. Without this clause, an early termination becomes an ad hoc negotiation exactly when goodwill is lowest.

### Limitation of liability
A cap on your liability (commonly capped at fees paid) — standard practice that protects a solo operator from exposure disproportionate to the size of the engagement.

### Dispute resolution & governing law
Which jurisdiction's law applies and how disputes get resolved (negotiation → mediation → arbitration/court) — decided calmly now instead of during an actual dispute.

## Where this sits in your document stack

This agreement should reference the Scope of Work by name (as an exhibit) so deliverables live in one place, and its payment terms should match whatever you send on the Invoice Template — three documents, one consistent story, no room for "that's not what I remember agreeing to."
