## Why the first week of a project is usually the slowest

Most delays in the first two weeks of an engagement aren't about the work — they're about waiting on access, approvals, or answers to logistics questions nobody asked up front. This questionnaire collects that information in one pass, before work starts, instead of in a slow drip of Slack messages during week one.

## Sections to include

### Stakeholders
- Who is the primary point of contact for day-to-day questions?
- Who has final sign-off authority on deliverables?
- Are there other people who need to be looped in, and on what?

### Access & assets
- What tools/systems will I need access to (and who grants it)?
- Where do existing brand assets, past work, or reference materials live?
- Are there any systems with a formal access-request process that takes days, not hours? (Ask this now — it's the single biggest hidden delay in most projects.)

### Goals & success criteria
- In one sentence, what does success look like for this engagement from your side?
- Is there a specific metric or outcome this will be measured against?
- Is there anything from a past vendor/freelancer engagement that didn't work, that you want done differently this time?

### Communication preferences
- Preferred channel (email, Slack, a shared doc) and expected response time
- Standing meeting cadence, if any
- Who should — and shouldn't — be copied on updates

### Approval process
- Who needs to approve deliverables before they're considered final?
- What's the typical turnaround time for feedback on your side?
- Is there a formal review/legal/compliance step that adds time we should build into the schedule?

### Logistics
- Invoicing details: billing contact, PO number if required, preferred payment method
- Any tools or file formats the final deliverables must be compatible with
- Time zone, if collaboration will be real-time

## How to use it

Send this immediately after the contract is signed, framed as "so I can hit the ground running rather than asking you piecemeal." Answers here should get cross-referenced against the Scope of Work — if a stated goal doesn't match what's in the SOW, that's a signal to clarify before work begins, not after.
