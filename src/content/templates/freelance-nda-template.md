## When a freelancer actually needs an NDA

Most freelancers only think about confidentiality once it's already in the service agreement — but by then, the sensitive part of the conversation may have already happened. A serious prospect will often share real numbers, internal strategy, or unreleased product details during your [discovery call](/templates/discovery-call-script) or while you're drafting a [consulting proposal](/templates/consulting-proposal-template), long before either of you has signed anything. An NDA sent ahead of that conversation is what makes it safe for both sides to talk openly that early.

## What a freelance NDA needs to cover

### A clear definition of confidential information
Broad enough to cover business, technical, financial, and strategic information disclosed in connection with the engagement — but not so broad that ordinary conversation becomes a legal risk for either party.

### Standard exclusions
Information that was already public, already known to the receiving party, or independently developed shouldn't be covered — without this carve-out, an NDA can become unenforceable or absurd to comply with.

### Mutual obligations
Both parties agree not to disclose or use the other's confidential information outside the purpose of evaluating or delivering the engagement. Mutuality matters here — you're not just protecting the client's secrets, you're protecting your own pricing model, process, and tools from being lifted and reused.

### A defined term
State how long the obligation lasts after disclosure — commonly one to three years. An NDA with no end date is harder to enforce and often gets pushback from more sophisticated clients.

### Return or destruction of information
What happens to shared materials if the relationship doesn't move forward — a short, low-friction clause that closes the loop cleanly if you end up not working together.

## Where an NDA sits in your document stack

An NDA is a pre-engagement document — it exists to make the conversations before a contract exists safe for both sides, then gets superseded by the confidentiality clause already built into your [freelance service agreement](/templates/freelance-contract-template) once the engagement actually begins. You don't need one for every casual inquiry; reach for it when a prospect is about to share something specific enough that either of you would care if it leaked — a product roadmap, financials, or a strategy that hasn't launched yet. Keep a signed copy on file for every client you send one to, the same way you'd keep a signed contract.

**A note on the legal side:** this template is a well-structured starting point, not legal advice. NDA enforceability varies by jurisdiction, and a lawyer's review is worth the cost before you rely on one for anything high-stakes.
