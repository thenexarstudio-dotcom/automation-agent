## What a retainer agreement actually protects

A retainer is not a discount on hourly work — it's the sale of guaranteed capacity. The client pays a fixed monthly fee to reserve a defined slice of your time and priority; you accept a slightly lower effective rate in exchange for income you can forecast months out. Almost every retainer that goes wrong goes wrong for the same reason: the agreement names a price but never precisely defines what that price buys. The result is a fixed fee attached to expanding expectations — the worst possible position for a freelancer.

## The clauses that make a retainer work

### Scope and the monthly cap
State exactly what the retainer covers: a number of hours, a set of recurring deliverables, or a defined type of work (e.g., "up to 15 hours of design support per month"). This is the single most important line in the document. Without a cap, "retained" quietly becomes "on call for anything," and your effective rate collapses. Anything beyond the cap routes through the [Change Order template](/templates/change-order-template) or is billed at your standard rate.

### Rollover and expiry
Decide whether unused hours roll over, and be conservative. Unlimited rollover lets a client bank three quiet months and then flood you in the fourth — which destroys the predictable capacity the retainer exists to create. A small, expiring rollover keeps things fair without handing over an open-ended obligation.

### Term, notice, and renewal
Set a minimum term (often three months), a notice period for cancellation (30 days is standard), and a scheduled rate-review clause. Building the review in from the start means future rate increases are a contractual formality, not an awkward renegotiation. If you're not sure what your retainer rate should be, work backward from a target using the [freelance day rate calculator](/tools/freelance-day-rate-calculator).

### Priority and response terms
Part of what a retainer buys is access. If you're promising faster turnaround or reserved availability, name it — response windows, priority over non-retainer clients — so the value is visible and the expectation is bounded.

### Payment timing
Retainers are paid in advance, at the start of each period. State the invoice date, the due date, and what happens if payment lapses (work pauses until the retainer is current). Advance payment is the entire cash-flow advantage of a retainer; billing in arrears throws it away.

## How it fits the rest of the Kit

A retainer agreement doesn't replace your [freelance contract](/templates/freelance-contract-template) — it sits on top of it, referencing the same terms for IP, confidentiality, and liability while adding the recurring-fee structure. The monthly cap should point at a lightweight [scope of work](/templates/scope-of-work-template) so both sides know what "in scope" means for the fee, and everything beyond it has a formal path through a change order rather than a Slack message nobody can find three months later.
