## What most free contract templates get wrong

Search for a free freelance contract template and you'll find plenty of options — but most are missing at least one of the clauses that actually matter in practice: a clear IP-ownership trigger tied to final payment, a specific late-payment mechanism, or a termination clause that says what's owed if the engagement ends early.

## What a strong freelance contract template should include

- **Clear IP ownership transfer** — tied to final payment received, not delivery, so unpaid work can't be used.
- **Specific payment terms** — due dates, accepted methods, and a stated late fee, not just "payment due upon invoice."
- **A termination clause** — notice period and what's owed for work completed to that point.
- **A liability cap** — commonly limited to fees paid, protecting a solo operator from disproportionate exposure.
- **A reference to a separate scope document** — so deliverables can be updated without redrafting the whole contract.
- **Confidentiality that runs both directions** — protecting client information and your own methods and materials.

## Where the free options usually fall short

Most free, generic contract templates are written for maximum applicability, which means they're vague exactly where freelance engagements need specificity — payment triggers, IP timing, and how scope changes get handled. That vagueness is where disputes happen.
