Retainers are sold as the holy grail of freelancing: predictable monthly income, no constant pitching. But a retainer is just a fixed fee divided by however many hours the client actually pulls from you — and clients pull more than they estimate. The moment those hours creep up, your effective rate quietly slides below what you'd earn on ordinary project work, and you don't notice because the deposit still lands every month.

This calculator makes that math visible. Enter the monthly fee, the hours the retainer really consumes, and your target hourly-equivalent rate. You'll get the effective rate the retainer actually pays, the gap versus your target in dollars and percent, and a plain verdict: above, at, or below your rate. If you're below, don't just accept it — cap the included hours or raise the fee. A retainer should buy the client priority and buy you stability, not silently discount your time.
