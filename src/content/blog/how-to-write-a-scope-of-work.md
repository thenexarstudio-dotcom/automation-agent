## Why vague scope invites vague requests

When "deliverables" is one paragraph instead of an itemized list, every new client request feels reasonable — because nothing on paper says otherwise. A specific scope of work isn't about being rigid with clients; it's about giving both sides the same reference point to check requests against.

## The five things every SOW needs

**1. Itemized deliverables.** Each deliverable as its own line, specific enough that both sides could independently check whether it's done — format, scale, and outcome, not a vague description.

**2. Explicit exclusions.** A short list of adjacent work you are *not* doing. This single section prevents more disputes than any other part of the document.

**3. A stated revision limit.** A specific number of rounds per deliverable (commonly two), and what happens past that — usually billed hourly or via a change order.

**4. Timeline with dependencies.** Key dates, and what you need from the client to hit them. Late client input is one of the most common causes of blown deadlines — name the dependency up front.

**5. A change process.** One paragraph describing exactly what happens when a request falls outside the document: a written request, a priced change order, approval before work begins.
