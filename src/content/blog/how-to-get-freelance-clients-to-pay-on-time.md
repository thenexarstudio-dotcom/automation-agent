## It's rarely about the client avoiding you

The most common reason a freelance invoice sits unpaid isn't that the client is dodging you — it's that the invoice is missing something their accounts payable process needs (a PO number, a specific due date, an itemized breakdown), so it sits in a queue until someone happens to chase it.

## Four fixes that matter more than a stern email

**1. Itemize every line.** Bill each deliverable or milestone as its own line, matching the language in your Scope of Work — not one vague "consulting services" line, which is processed slower and disputed more.

**2. Ask for a PO number during onboarding.** If a client's process requires one, get it before the project starts, via your Client Onboarding Questionnaire — not after the first invoice bounces back.

**3. State a late fee, every time.** A stated late fee (commonly 1.5–2% per month), printed on the invoice itself and matching what's in your Service Agreement, rarely gets triggered often — but its presence alone measurably improves on-time payment rates.

**4. Invoice the same day, every time.** Send the invoice the moment a milestone is approved, not batched at month-end. Prompt invoicing is one of the single biggest levers on how fast you get paid, more than the exact wording of your payment terms.

## Put it in the contract too

Payment terms should appear in both the [Freelance Service Agreement Template](/templates/freelance-contract-template) and every [Invoice Template](/templates/invoice-template) you send — consistent language in both places removes any ambiguity about what was agreed.
