## The three ways proposals lose deals

Weak proposals tend to fail in one of three ways: they restate generic capabilities instead of the client's specific problem, they hide the price several pages in, or they end with no clear next step. Fixing all three matters more than any amount of polish on the writing itself.

## The seven sections that work

1. **Situation summary** — the client's problem, in their own words, in two or three sentences.
2. **Proposed approach** — three to five specific bullet points on how you'll solve it, not generic capability statements.
3. **Deliverables** — concrete, nameable artifacts, not vague outcomes.
4. **Timeline** — a simple table: phase, deliverable, date.
5. **Investment** — the fee, framed as a project investment, with payment terms attached directly.
6. **What's excluded** — a short paragraph naming what's out of scope, which prevents more scope disputes later than anything in the contract.
7. **Next step** — one sentence, one clear action, one deadline.

## Send it fast, follow up on a schedule

Send the proposal within 24 hours of the sales conversation, while urgency is highest. If there's no response, a short, specific follow-up at day 3 and day 7 recovers a meaningful share of deals that would otherwise quietly die in an inbox.
