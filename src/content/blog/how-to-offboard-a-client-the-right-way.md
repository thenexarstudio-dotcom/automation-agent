## Most engagements end the same underwhelming way

Final deliverable sent, final invoice sent, silence. It's the default, and it quietly wastes the single highest-leverage moment in the entire client relationship: right after a successful delivery, when the client's enthusiasm is at its peak.

## What that moment is worth

Two things are dramatically easier to get right after a successful delivery than at any other point: a specific, usable testimonial, and a direct referral. Both are best asked for before the final invoice is paid — not after, when the conversation has moved on.

## A short, specific ask beats a generic one

"Would you write me a review?" produces vague, low-value testimonials. "Would you be willing to speak to how [specific outcome] played out?" produces something you can actually use. The same logic applies to referrals: "if you know one or two other [role] dealing with [problem you solved], I'd appreciate an intro" gets answered far more often than "let me know if you know anyone."

## Make it a checklist, not a hope

The reason most freelancers skip this isn't that they don't value referrals — it's that there's no prompt at the moment a project winds down.
