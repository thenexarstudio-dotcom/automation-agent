## r/freelance — angle: the day-rate math post

**Title:** The billable-days math most freelancers get wrong (you have ~120, not 220)

**Body:**

A calendar year has about 260 working days, so a lot of freelancers price as if they can bill most of them. Then admin, sales calls, proposals, learning, sick days, and vacation eat 100+ of those days — and the rate that looked fine on paper quietly becomes minimum wage.

The honest math: income target + overhead, divided by *realistic* billable days (100–140 for most solo folks). That number is usually 50-80% higher than what people are actually charging.

I put a free calculator online that does this (no signup, runs in-browser, doesn't collect anything): [link to /tools/freelance-day-rate-calculator]

Curious what others get when they run honest numbers — was your current rate above or below?

## r/consulting — angle: the scope-creep receipt

**Title:** I calculated what unbilled "quick additions" actually cost over a year and it's ugly

**Body:**

Three unbilled extra hours a week at a $85/hr effective rate is $255/week. Over an 8-week project that's ~$2k. If the pattern holds across a year of engagements, it's $11k+ of work done for free — usually without a single conversation about it, because each individual request felt too small to push back on.

The fix isn't saying no more — it's having a one-page change order ready to send the same day, so "can you also add X" gets a friendly yes-with-a-price instead of a silent unpaid yes.

Free calculator if you want to see your own number: [link to /tools/scope-creep-cost-calculator]

## Indie Hackers — angle: build-in-public / portfolio business

**Title:** Building a digital products portfolio for the freelance ops niche — what's live after the build sprint

**Body:**

Sharing the stack and strategy since IH folks ask about digital products businesses a lot.

The thesis: freelancers don't need another SaaS subscription — they need better *documents* (proposals, scopes, contracts, invoices). Search demand for "[x] template" / "[x] calculator" in this niche is steady and commercial.
